The problem it solves

The desk slump is sneaky. It never announces itself.

Nobody decides to sit badly for four hours. It just happens — a meeting runs long, a ticket needs finishing, and suddenly the afternoon is gone and your body has been folded into one position the whole time.

  • ✓ Your neck eases forward toward the screen and the muscles at the back of your skull stay switched-on for hours.
  • ✓ Your shoulders creep up toward your ears during focused work, turning mild tension into an actual ache by evening.
  • ✓ Your lower back holds the same seated curve long after it’s stopped feeling comfortable, because you were too deep in flow to notice.
  • ✓ Your wrists and hands stay locked in tiny repetitive motions that never get a counter-motion to balance them.

You didn’t even notice three hours passed.

The fix isn’t willpower — it’s a cue. Deskercise decides for you, then makes the next step so small there’s no excuse to skip it.

Get started

One line, then let it run.

Install globally with your package manager of choice, then start a session. Deskercise lives in your terminal and keeps working quietly in the background of your day.

$ npm install --global @thulioph/deskercise
Requires Node 22.13+ macOS only Runs entirely offline

Prefer a manual install? Grab a tarball from GitHub Releases.

Two commands

Start a break rhythm. Check how you did.

Start a session

Begins the 45-minute timer and walks you through each break when it arrives. Skip or snooze if you’re mid-meeting — it won’t guilt you, it’ll just come back.

deskercise start

See your week

Opens a local HTML report straight from your home directory — a log of completed breaks, skips, and confirmed minutes. It never leaves your machine.

The studies behind it

Movement breaks aren’t a new idea — they’re just hard to remember.

Reference

Newcastle Hospitals NHS

The “1 minute body check” — a plain reminder that brief, regular check-ins with your posture and movement add up over a shift.

Reference

CCOHS

Stretching at the workstation — guidance on small, desk-friendly stretches that target the muscles desk work overworks most.

Reference

Mersey Care NHS

Desk-based jobs — practical advice on staying comfortable and mobile during long stretches of seated computer work.

The 45-minute interval and 60-second break window are Deskercise’s own product choices — not recommendations from these organizations. Their work simply informed the idea that regular micro-breaks are worth doing.

Your data

Local-first. Private by design.

Deskercise works only on your machine. There are no accounts to create, no telemetry to opt out of, and no network requests while it runs. Your break history belongs to you.

Everything it needs lives in a single hidden folder in your home directory. Delete that folder and Deskercise is gone, clean and complete.
